and the Return to Bayesian Competence}
\author{Research note}
\date{July 2026}

\begin{document}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
This note generalizes the four-state laboratory to an arbitrary finite number
of agents, a general finite state space, and stochastic consequences.  The
architecture follows the subjective-rationality tradition: each agent adopts a
causal theory, combines it with beliefs and conjectures to choose a committed
policy, and the profile of policies determines everyone's consequences through
an objective causal system.  All market and environmental detail is absorbed
into that system; the front end---awareness, dissonance, inquiry, insight, and
the reconstruction of belief---parallels the laboratory.  The organizing
object is the chain
\[
\text{dissonance}\longrightarrow\text{inquiry}\longrightarrow
\text{refined awareness}\longrightarrow\text{new models}\longrightarrow
\text{Bayesian judgment},
\]
the process by which an agent whose causal language has failed regains the
ability to be a Bayesian.  Theories are assignments of elementary causal
mechanisms to awareness cells; dissonance is the statistical rejection of
every expressible assignment; inquiry is a costly, delabeled election; insight
is a fallible refinement of the partition; and the bridge prior restores a
Bayesian posture on the expanded language.  A self-confirming unawareness
equilibrium is defined not as a strategic rest point but as the configuration
in which every agent permanently inhabits a Bayesian world: models fit,
questions are dead, and inquiry is rationally unpriced.  Convergence to such
configurations, their possible falsity off the exposed path, and the
persistence of performance differences follow from the economics of the
process rather than from an equilibrium postulate.
\end{abstract}

\section{Purpose, fulcrum, and relation to the laboratory}
\label{sec:purpose}

This note supplies the general canvas for the ideas made concrete in the
four-state laboratory \citep{FourStateNote}.  Its subject is the process by
which a strategic agent whose causal representation has failed regains the
capacity for ordinary Bayesian reasoning: experience rejects every expressible
theory; the rejection localizes a question; costly inquiry may produce a
previously inexpressible distinction; the expanded language admits new
theories; and belief formation resumes.  Equilibrium concepts appear only at
the end, and only in a subordinate role: a self-confirming unawareness
equilibrium describes the configuration in which this process has permanently
terminated---every agent lives in a Bayesian world and has no represented
reason to leave it.  Persistence of theory heterogeneity and of performance
differences is derived from the economics of the process: from what inquiry
costs, what questions are worth, which experiences the play of the game
supplies, and where refinement paths lead.

The back end follows the subjective-rationality tradition of
\citet{KalaiLehrer1993b} and \citet{Ryall2003}.  A finite set of agents adopt
causal theories; theories, beliefs, and conjectures determine committed
policies; and the policy profile determines stochastic consequences for every
agent through an objective causal system.  Everything about markets,
customers, and technology is absorbed into that system.  The note adds to this
tradition the front end: the agents' \emph{language} for the system---which
distinctions they can represent---is endogenous, and the fixed-hypothesis-space
model is recovered as the special case in which awareness never changes.

Two disciplines are maintained throughout.  First, causality: the objective
system specifies the distribution of consequences that \emph{would} result
from every action profile at every state, whether or not that profile is ever
played.  Agents' actions cause their consequences; theories are causal models
of that relation; and the gap between on-path evidence and counterfactual
content is what the identification results bite on.  No structural-causal-model
formalism is imposed---mechanisms are reduced-form causal objects---but
detailed micro-models can generate them in examples, as the laboratory's
winner-take-all customer does.  Second, the no-baked-in checklist of the
companion memorandum: nothing behind an agent's awareness frontier may carry
semantic content or objective payoff information into pre-insight
deliberation.

\section{Primitives}
\label{sec:primitives}

\subsection{Agents, states, actions, consequences}

There are $n$ agents, $N=\{1,\dots,n\}$.  Time is divided into generations
$g=1,2,\dots$, each consisting of $T$ episodes.  In every episode a state
$s\in S$ is drawn independently from a full-support distribution
$\rho\in\Delta(S)$; each agent takes an action $a_i\in A_i$ (through the
committed policy defined below); and a consequence profile
$x=(x_1,\dots,x_n)\in X$ is realized.  Agent $i$ receives payoff $u_i(x_i)$.
The consequence $x_i$ is everything agent $i$ obtains from the episode's
outcome---profit, sales, observed reactions---and payoffs are functions of
consequences rather than separate primitives.

\subsection{Mechanisms and the objective causal system}
\label{sec:mechanisms}

\begin{definition}[Elementary mechanisms and the objective system]
\label{def:mechanisms}
A finite set $\Theta$ of \emph{elementary causal mechanisms} is given, each a
map $\theta:A\to\Delta(X)$.  The \emph{objective assignment} is a function
$\tau:S\to\Theta$, and the \emph{objective causal system} is
\begin{equation}
F(\cdot\mid s,a)=\tau(s)(a)\in\Delta(X).
\label{eq:objective-system}
\end{equation}
Two states are \emph{causally equivalent} if they carry the same mechanism.
Write $\mathcal P^{\tau}$ for the partition of $S$ into causal-equivalence
classes and $\marg{i}\theta(a)$ for the $X_i$-marginal of $\theta(a)$.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Causal reading]
\label{rem:causal-reading}
The system $F$ is a response function, not a statistical summary: it specifies
the distribution of consequences that would result from \emph{every} action
profile at every state, including profiles never played.  Agents' actions,
together with the state, cause consequences; a mechanism is the reduced form
of whatever detailed model---a structural causal model of customers, a market
microstructure, a production process---underlies that relation, and examples
may exhibit such micro-foundations explicitly.  Three consequences of the
causal reading recur below.  First, theories (assignments of mechanisms to
cells) are interventional objects: a theory makes predictions at unplayed
profiles.  Second, on-path data constrain $F$ only at visited state--profile
pairs, so observationally equivalent systems with different counterfactual
content are generic; that is the engine of the nonidentification results.
Third, the on-path conditional distribution of consequences given an
awareness cell is a \emph{selection object}---it mixes mechanisms according to
which states co-occur with which play---and must not be conflated with any
mechanism's own conditional.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Why theories are mechanism assignments]
\label{rem:why-mechanisms}
If expressible theories were arbitrary conditional distributions over
consequences per cell, any within-cell mixture would itself be expressible
and no experience could reject the whole class: coarseness alone cannot
produce dissonance in a saturated model space.  The laboratory avoided this
because its theories were the structured labels $\{0,1,n\}$.  The general
counterpart is $\Theta$: an awareness cell is the agent's implicit claim that
its states are causally alike, an expressible theory assigns one elementary
mechanism to each cell, and dissonance---defined below---is statistical
evidence of \emph{mechanism heterogeneity inside a represented category}.
What insight produces is the distinction that splits the category; the
mechanism vocabulary $\Theta$ itself is known to all agents from the outset,
exactly as the labels were in the laboratory.  Expansion of the mechanism
vocabulary is a further, deeper dimension of awareness growth deliberately
left outside this paper.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Awareness, theories, conjectures, and policies}
\label{sec:awareness}

\begin{definition}[Awareness and expressible theories]
\label{def:awareness}
Agent $i$'s awareness partition is $\mathcal P_i\in\Pi(S)$.  Every consciously
available object---theory, belief, conjecture, policy, question---must be
measurable with respect to $\mathcal P_i$.  The expressible theories are the
cell-constant mechanism assignments
\begin{equation}
M(\mathcal P_i)=\{m:\mathcal P_i\to\Theta\},
\label{eq:expressible}
\end{equation}
with $m$ predicting $x_i\sim\marg{i}m(C)(a)$ at cell $C$ under profile $a$.
The objective assignment $\tau$ is expressible for agent $i$ if and only if
$\tau$ is constant on every cell of $\mathcal P_i$, that is, if and only if
$\mathcal P_i$ refines $\mathcal P^{\tau}$; call such a partition
\emph{sufficient} for $\tau$.
\end{definition}

Agent $i$ holds a belief $\mu_i\in\Delta(M(\mathcal P_i))$ over expressible
theories and a conjecture $q_i:\mathcal P_i\to\Delta(A_{-i})$ about rival
play, both $\mathcal P_i$-measurable.  Theories and conjectures are different
kinds of objects and are deliberately separated: a theory is a causal claim
about the objective system; a conjecture is a strategic claim about what other
agents will do.  Dissonance, defined below, tests theories, not conjectures.

\begin{assumption}[Observation and retention]
\label{ass:observation}
At the end of each episode, agent $i$ observes the triple
$(C_{\mathcal P_i}(s),a,x_i)$: its cell, the realized action profile, and its
own consequence.  Episodes are retained as tokens in a deliberative record
$r_i$ and can be retrospectively recoded after the agent acquires a new
distinction; before acquisition, neither belief nor action can condition on
that distinction.  The analyst-level history additionally records $s$, so that
a newly produced representation can recode the tokens.
\end{assumption}

Observability of the full profile is the laboratory's convention and keeps the
separation of causal from strategic error clean; a variant in which components
of $a$ are folded into the consequence $x_i$ (so that $x_i$ is literally
everything seen) changes bookkeeping but nothing conceptual.

\begin{assumption}[Commitment and subjective optimality]
\label{ass:commitment}
At the start of each generation, agent $i$ commits to a policy
$\sigma_i:\mathcal P_i\to A_i$ maximizing subjective expected payoff,
\begin{equation}
\sigma_i(C)\in\arg\max_{a_i\in A_i}
\sum_{m\in M(\mathcal P_i)}\mu_i(m)
\sum_{a_{-i}\in A_{-i}}q_i(a_{-i}\mid C)\,
\mathbb E_{x\sim m(C)(a_i,a_{-i})}\!\left[u_i(x_i)\right],
\label{eq:subjective-optimality}
\end{equation}
holds the policy for the generation's $T$ episodes, and updates only between
generations.  When dissonance has made $\mu_i$ undefined and no new
representation has been produced, the previously committed policy is carried
forward.  Agents evaluate one generation ahead; no discounting is imposed.
\end{assumption}

Commitment is where the statistical structure and the economics meet: a test
needs a stable data-generating configuration, and a generation of committed
play supplies exactly that.  What the laboratory assumed as a batching device
is, in the stochastic model, the natural granularity of learning.

\subsection{On-path conditionals}

Fix a generation with committed profile $\sigma=(\sigma_1,\dots,\sigma_n)$.
Each state $s$ receives the deterministic profile
$a(s)=(\sigma_j(C_{\mathcal P_j}(s)))_{j\in N}$.  Agent $i$'s \emph{on-path
conditional} at an observation pair $(C,a)$ with
$S(C,a)=\{s\in C:a(s)=a\}\neq\emptysetset$ is
\begin{equation}
p_i^{*}(\cdot\mid C,a)
=
\sum_{s\in S(C,a)}\rho\bigl(s\mid S(C,a)\bigr)\,\marg{i}\tau(s)(a).
\label{eq:onpath-conditional}
\end{equation}
This is the selection object of \cref{rem:causal-reading}: rivals with finer
partitions condition on distinctions $i$ cannot see, so the within-cell state
mixture can vary with the profile.  It is precisely this dependence---
differentiated play by others inside one's own cell---that generates the
contrasts on which dissonance feeds.

\section{The Bayesian layer and its boundary}
\label{sec:bayesian-layer}

Within fixed awareness, learning is ordinary.  Observations representable in
the current language update $\mu_i$ by Bayes' rule; conjectures are updated
empirically from observed play.  This layer nests the fixed-vocabulary
subjective-rationality models: if no agent's awareness ever changes, the model
is a repeated interaction among subjectively rational Bayesian learners in the
sense of \citet{KalaiLehrer1993b} and \citet{Ryall2003}.

\begin{proposition}[No Bayesian emergence]
\label{prop:no-emergence}
Bayesian conditioning on any event measurable with respect to the algebra
generated by $\mathcal P_i$ yields a posterior on $\Delta(M(\mathcal P_i))$.
No sequence of such updates makes a theory outside $M(\mathcal P_i)$
expressible or a distinction finer than $\mathcal P_i$ usable in a policy.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Conditioning reweights a distribution on its domain and cannot enlarge the
domain; every element of $M(\mathcal P_i)$ is cell-constant, so every
posterior mixture and every policy optimal against it is
$\mathcal P_i$-measurable.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The posterior is self-sealing]
\label{rem:self-sealing}
The stochastic setting adds a sharper point.  Suppose every expressible theory
is false---the cell mixes mechanisms, so no cell-constant assignment matches
the on-path conditionals.  A full-support Bayesian posterior does not signal
this: by the logic of misspecified Bayesian learning \citep{Berk1966}, the
posterior concentrates on the expressible theories closest to the truth in
Kullback--Leibler divergence, and the agent becomes increasingly
\emph{confident} in a wrong theory.  Posterior mass always sums to one; model
failure is not an event in the agent's algebra.  Detecting that the language
itself is broken therefore requires a faculty outside the posterior---the
consistency test of the next section.  This is the formal counterpart of
separating understanding from judgment: updating ranks the answers one can
express, while judgment asks whether any expressible answer is adequate.
\end{remark}

\section{Tests, active sets, and statistical dissonance}
\label{sec:dissonance}

\subsection{The consistency test}

\begin{definition}[Consistency test and active set]
\label{def:test}
Fix a tolerance $\eta\geq0$, a minimum count $\bar n\geq1$, and a
nonincreasing slack sequence $w(\cdot)\to0$.  Given record $r_i$ with counts
$n_i(C,a)$ and empirical conditionals $\hat p_i(\cdot\mid C,a)$, theory
$m\in M(\mathcal P_i)$ \emph{passes} if
\begin{equation}
\TV\bigl(\hat p_i(\cdot\mid C,a),\,\marg{i}m(C)(a)\bigr)
\leq
\eta+w\bigl(n_i(C,a)\bigr)
\qquad
\text{for every }(C,a)\text{ with }n_i(C,a)\geq\bar n.
\label{eq:test}
\end{equation}
The \emph{active set} $\widehat M_i(\mathcal P_i,r_i)$ is the set of passing
theories.  Call $m$ \emph{$\eta$-adequate} under a committed profile if
$\TV(p_i^{*}(\cdot\mid C,a),\marg{i}m(C)(a))\leq\eta$ at every visited pair,
and \emph{$\eta$-inadequate} if the inequality fails strictly at some visited
pair.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Test consistency]
\label{lem:test-consistency}
Fix a committed profile played in all generations and suppose the tolerance is
generic in the sense that no theory's distance to an on-path conditional
equals $\eta$ exactly.  As generations accumulate, almost surely: every
$\eta$-adequate theory is eventually always in the active set, and every
$\eta$-inadequate theory is eventually never in it.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Visited pairs have $n_i(C,a)\to\infty$ almost surely under i.i.d.\ full-support
states, and $\hat p_i(\cdot\mid C,a)\to p_i^{*}(\cdot\mid C,a)$ by the strong
law.  The triangle inequality then places
$\TV(\hat p_i,\marg{i}m(C)(a))$ within any $\epsilon$ of
$\TV(p_i^{*},\marg{i}m(C)(a))$ eventually; genericity converts the strict
inequalities into eventual acceptance and rejection, and the slack term
absorbs finite-sample fluctuation.
\end{proof}

The deterministic laboratory is the degenerate instance $\eta=0$, $\bar n=1$,
$w\equiv0$ with deterministic mechanisms: passing is exact consistency, and
the active set is the laboratory's.

\begin{definition}[Causal dissonance]
\label{def:dissonance}
Agent $i$ experiences \emph{causal dissonance} at the end of a generation if
$\widehat M_i(\mathcal P_i,r_i)=\emptysetset$: every expressible theory is
rejected.  The expressible set does not disappear; no element of it accounts
for the record.  The immediate question has a determinate defect but not a
determinate answer: what unrepresented difference among the pooled states
explains the heterogeneity of their consequences?
\end{definition}

\subsection{Exposure}

Dissonance requires more than falsity; the play of the game must make the
falsity visible.

\begin{definition}[Exposure]
\label{def:exposure}
Under a committed profile, awareness $\mathcal P_i$ is \emph{exposed} if every
$m\in M(\mathcal P_i)$ is $\eta$-inadequate.  A committed profile is
\emph{concealing} for agent $i$ if some expressible theory is
$\eta$-adequate under it despite being objectively false---false, that is, as
a claim about $\tau$ at some state its cells cover.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Exposure and dissonance]
\label{prop:exposure}
Under a fixed committed profile, agent $i$ experiences dissonance from some
generation onward almost surely if and only if $\mathcal P_i$ is exposed.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Immediate from \cref{lem:test-consistency}: exposure means every theory is
eventually rejected; its failure means some theory is eventually always
accepted.
\end{proof}

Exposure generalizes the laboratory's revelation lemma and its demand-side
extension in one object.  There, pooled play under forgiving demand concealed
the row law from column believers; harsh demand or differentiated rivals
exposed it.  Here, whether heterogeneity inside a cell surfaces depends on
which profiles are visited (strategic differentiation inside one's cells) and
on how sharply mechanisms differ at the visited profiles (the environment's
harshness).  Both sources of diagnosticity reappear in the dynamics of
\cref{sec:dynamics}.

\section{Inquiry: restoration, direction, and fallibility}
\label{sec:inquiry}

\subsection{Restorative refinements}

For $\mathcal P,\mathcal P'\in\Pi(S)$, write $\mathcal P\prec\mathcal P'$ when
$\mathcal P'$ strictly refines $\mathcal P$.  After a refinement, the retained
record is recoded (\cref{ass:observation}) and the test is re-applied with the
recoded counts.

\begin{definition}[Restorative refinement]
\label{def:restorative}
$\mathcal P'$ is \emph{restorative} at $(\mathcal P_i,r_i)$ if
$\mathcal P_i\prec\mathcal P'$ and
$\widehat M_i(\mathcal P',r_i)\neq\emptysetset$ under the recoded record.  It
is \emph{minimally restorative} if no restorative $\mathcal P''$ satisfies
$\mathcal P_i\prec\mathcal P''\prec\mathcal P'$.  Write
$\mathcal R(\mathcal P_i,r_i)$ for the set of minimally restorative
refinements.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Cellwise coherence]
\label{prop:coherence}
Call a cell $C'$ of a refinement \emph{coherent} if some single mechanism
passes the test on $C'$'s recoded observations.  Then
$\widehat M_i(\mathcal P',r_i)\neq\emptysetset$ if and only if every cell of
$\mathcal P'$ is coherent.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Theories factor across cells---$M(\mathcal P')=\Theta^{\mathcal P'}$ and the
test constraints in \cref{eq:test} are per cell---so a passing assignment
exists exactly when a passing mechanism can be chosen cell by cell.
\end{proof}

Coherence generalizes the laboratory's conflict-free condition.  There,
incoherence was a binary relation between two labeled episodes and restorative
partitions were proper colorings of a conflict graph; here, incoherence is in
general a property of \emph{sets} of tokens (a mixture of three mechanisms may
be incompatible with every single mechanism even though every pair of tokens
is individually accommodatable), so restoration is a hypergraph-coloring
problem.  Two structural facts survive unchanged: restoration is always
possible (the partition into singletons recodes every token to its own state,
where a single mechanism always fits a single-state record), and minimal
restorations are generally multiple---the record directs inquiry without
determining its output.

\subsection{The technology}

\begin{definition}[Representation-producing technology]
\label{def:technology}
A representation-producing technology for agent $i$ is a (possibly randomized)
map $\phi_i$ from $(\mathcal P_i,r_i)$ to $\Pi(S)$ with
$\phi_i(\mathcal P_i,r_i)\in\{\mathcal P_i\}\cup
\mathcal R(\mathcal P_i,r_i)$: it fails, or it succeeds and produces a
minimally restorative refinement.  It is \emph{truth-blind}: its output
distribution depends only on $(\mathcal P_i,r_i)$, never directly on $\tau$.
A complexity ordering $\kappa$ on refinements---the analyst's model of which
conceptual products the agent's comparison capacities can generate---may
restrict the range further, as the coordinate-complexity filter did in the
laboratory; $\kappa$ is a primitive of the technology, not an object of the
agent's optimization.
\end{definition}

\begin{assumption}[Dissonance-triggered, costly, elective production]
\label{ass:elective}
$\phi_i$ operates only when agent $i$ experiences dissonance and elects
inquiry, $\varepsilon_i=1$, at cost $\gamma_i>0$.  Agent $i$ holds a delabeled
success weight $\lambda_i\in(0,1]$: a subjective probability that an elected
episode produces a usable refinement, defined on the event of success alone
and not on the identities of possible outputs.  On failure or abstention the
committed policy is carried forward.  Awareness is cumulative: acquired
distinctions are never lost, though theories formulated on them may be
rejected.
\end{assumption}

\subsection{Nonidentification and the price of robustness}

\begin{theorem}[Observational nonidentification]
\label{thm:nonidentification}
Let $\tau\neq\tau'$ be objective assignments that induce the same distribution
over agent $i$'s records under the committed profiles played.  Then any
truth-blind technology and any truth-blind inquiry-and-formulation rule has
the same output distribution in both worlds.  If, in addition, no single
theory is $\eta$-adequate under both worlds at the visited pairs, then no
truth-blind rule selects an adequate formulation with probability one in both
worlds.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The rule's input is the record, whose distribution is identical by hypothesis;
its output distribution is therefore identical.  If the adequate sets are
disjoint, any output is inadequate in at least one world with the
corresponding probability.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Robust sufficiency]
\label{prop:robust-sufficiency}
A partition sufficient for every assignment in a candidate set
$\mathcal T$ must refine $\bigvee_{\tau'\in\mathcal T}\mathcal P^{\tau'}$, the
join of the candidates' causal-equivalence partitions.  Representations
guaranteed to accommodate all observationally equivalent worlds are
accordingly finer than any single world requires, and a technology that
economizes on distinctions must accept fallibility.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Sufficiency for $\tau'$ is refinement of $\mathcal P^{\tau'}$
(\cref{def:awareness}); refinement of each is refinement of the join.
\end{proof}

In the laboratory, the join of the row and column worlds was the singleton
partition; \cref{prop:robust-sufficiency} is that result in general form.

\section{The economics of inquiry}
\label{sec:inquiry-economics}

The election $\varepsilon_i$ must be priced at exactly the moment Bayes is
silent: the posterior is undefined at dissonance
(\cref{def:dissonance}), and the candidate answers are not represented
objects (\cref{def:technology}).  The laboratory's solution generalizes with
one qualification.

\begin{definition}[Pinned distinctions]
\label{def:pinned}
Token sets $K,K'\subseteq r_i$ are \emph{mutually incoherent} if no single
mechanism passes the test on $K\cup K'$.  A \emph{pinned distinction} is a
separation of mutually incoherent token sets.  Every restorative refinement
separates every pinned distinction---a cell containing both sets would be
incoherent (\cref{prop:coherence})---so conditioning future play on a pinned
distinction is a capability that \emph{any} successful inquiry outcome
delivers.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Retained-record leverage]
\label{def:leverage}
Under the subjective recurrence premise of the laboratory---the agent
evaluates one generation ahead as if its retained record recurs token for
token---let $B_i(r_i)$ be the largest per-generation payoff improvement over
the carried-forward policy that is achievable by a contingent rule measurable
with respect to pinned distinctions, computable from the record together with
the known structure $(\Theta,u_i)$, and robust to every rival behavior
consistent with the record.  Set $B_i=0$ when there is no dissonance.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Answer invariance and participation]
\label{prop:participation}
$B_i(r_i)$ is computable from the deliberative record alone and is identical
across all elements of $\mathcal R(\mathcal P_i,r_i)$: the agent prices the
question without a menu of answers.  Electing inquiry has represented value
$\lambda_iB_i(r_i)-\gamma_i$, so agent $i$ elects if and only if
$\lambda_iB_i(r_i)\geq\gamma_i$.  Moreover $B_i>0$ only if some pinned
distinction changes the robustly optimal action at some recurrence: questions
whose every admissible answer supports the current action are worthless at any
positive cost, however much model multiplicity remains.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Measurability with respect to pinned distinctions is deliverable by every
restorative refinement (\cref{def:pinned}), so the maximand in
\cref{def:leverage} does not depend on which refinement arrives; the value of
electing follows as in the laboratory, with the extrapolated component of any
full formulation assigned no represented value at the election.  If no pinned
distinction changes the robustly optimal action, every admissible contingent
rule is payoff-equivalent to the carried-forward policy on the recurrence
record, so the maximum is zero.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Exactness is special; opacity suppresses inquiry]
\label{rem:leverage-qualified}
In the laboratory at $\beta=1$, the constant-sum two-action structure made the
unit gain of a pinned separation exactly one-half against every rival action.
Generally the robust bound is not exact, and---more interestingly---it can be
zero even when refinement would objectively pay, because computing forgone
payoffs from the record requires the consequence space to be
\emph{counterfactually transparent}: rich enough that observed consequences,
together with the known mechanism family, identify what other own-actions
would have yielded.  Winner-take-all markets are transparent; environments
whose consequences conceal forgone values depress $B_i$ toward zero and hence
rationally suppress inquiry.  How much an environment lets agents price their
own questions is itself a dimension of diagnosticity, alongside exposure.
\end{remark}

\section{The pivot: formulation, warrant, and the return to Bayes}
\label{sec:pivot}

Successful production yields $\mathcal P_i'$; formulation selects
$m'\in\widehat M_i(\mathcal P_i',r_i)$; and the agent forms a full-support
bridge prior $\mu_i^{+}\in\Delta(M(\mathcal P_i'))$, from which ordinary
updating resumes.  Nothing in Bayesian coherence determines $\mu_i^{+}$ from
the pre-dissonance belief---the domains differ---and the renormalization of
$\mu_i^{+}$ on the active set is \emph{representation-relative coherence},
not warrant.  The laboratory's accounting of the difference generalizes.

\begin{definition}[Reflective assessment, warrant, and the gap]
\label{def:warrant}
A reflective assessment problem is a pair $(\mathcal T,\nu)$: a finite set of
candidate objective assignments with a full-support reflective prior.  Given
the record and a successful production-and-formulation event, the
\emph{selection-adjusted warrant} $W$ of $(\mathcal P_i',m')$ is the
$\nu$-posterior probability that $m'$ is objectively correct---that
$\tau'(s)=m'(C_{\mathcal P_i'}(s))$ for all $s$---conditional on the record
\emph{and} the production event.  The \emph{warrant gap} $G$ is the
representation-relative coherence probability of $m'$ minus $W$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[The insight is not evidence]
\label{thm:uninformative-production}
If the technology is truth-blind, conditioning on the production event adds
nothing to the record: $W$ equals the ordinary reflective posterior of $m'$'s
correctness given the record alone.  Moreover, post-restoration fit is
guaranteed by the restoration property, so passing the test carries no
information beyond the record either.  Among candidates in $\mathcal T$ that
induce the same record distribution, prior odds are preserved exactly.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The reflective posterior on $\tau'\in\mathcal T$ is proportional to
$\nu(\tau')$ times the record's likelihood under $\tau'$ times the probability
of the production event given the record; truth blindness makes the last
factor a function of the record alone, constant across $\mathcal T$, so it
cancels.  Fit is a deterministic function of record and output
(\cref{def:restorative}), hence likelihood one in every world consistent with
the event.  The final claim is the cancellation applied to candidates with
identical record likelihoods.
\end{proof}

Unlike the deterministic laboratory, the record itself is generally
informative among candidates---stochastic likelihoods differ---so warrant need
not equal the prior.  The theorem's content is the location of the boundary:
everything evidential is in the record; nothing evidential is in the arrival
or the retrospective success of the insight.  If production were correlated
with the objective assignment---social transmission from an informed
source---the event would carry information, and the adjustment could move
confidence in either direction.

\begin{corollary}[Self-audit requires sufficiency for the candidates]
\label{cor:self-audit}
The assessment $(\mathcal T,\nu)$ is expressible by agent $i$ only if
$\mathcal P_i$ is sufficient for every $\tau'\in\mathcal T$, hence only if
$\mathcal P_i$ refines the join of \cref{prop:robust-sufficiency}.  An agent
holding a minimal restoration therefore generally cannot formulate the
comparison that measures its own warrant gap; short of that refinement,
warrant must be supplied externally---by fresh evidence whose likelihoods
differ across candidates, by an assessor with broader awareness, or by a
future self.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Entertaining $\tau'$ as a hypothesis requires expressing it, which is
sufficiency (\cref{def:awareness}); sufficiency for all candidates is
refinement of the join.
\end{proof}

The pivot is now complete, and it is the note's fulcrum: dissonance broke the
agent's Bayesian competence; inquiry produced a distinction; formulation and
the bridge prior rebuilt a probability space; and judgment---fresh evidence
with differential likelihoods, honestly accounted---resumes as ordinary
updating on the expanded language.  The agent has returned to the Bayesian
world, carrying a warrant gap of known location and, in general, unknown
(to it) size.

\section{Dynamics: when everyone lives in a Bayesian world}
\label{sec:dynamics}

The chain now runs inside a population: theories determine policies, policies
determine which conditionals are visited, visited conditionals determine
dissonance and leverage, and refinements feed back into theories.  This
section characterizes where the process comes to rest.  The interesting
object is not the rest configuration as an equilibrium but what it asserts
epistemically: \emph{a state of the population in which every agent
permanently inhabits a Bayesian world}---models fit, questions are dead, and
no represented calculation prices further inquiry above its cost.

\begin{lemma}[Finitely many refinements]
\label{lem:finite-refinements}
Each successful production strictly increases the number of cells of some
agent's partition, so along any path there are at most $n(|S|-1)$ successful
productions.  Almost surely there is a last one.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
A strict refinement of a partition of a finite set adds at least one cell,
and cell counts are bounded by $|S|$; cumulative awareness
(\cref{ass:elective}) rules out coarsening.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Self-confirming unawareness equilibrium]
\label{def:scue}
A profile
$\bigl(\mathcal P_i,\mu_i,q_i,\sigma_i\bigr)_{i\in N}$ is a
\emph{self-confirming unawareness equilibrium} (SCUE) if, under the induced
play,
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item each $\sigma_i$ is subjectively optimal
  (\cref{eq:subjective-optimality});
  \item each conjecture $q_i$ agrees with the play induced by
  $\sigma_{-i}$ at every on-path cell;
  \item each agent's active set is almost surely eventually nonempty and each
  $\mu_i$ is supported on $\eta$-adequate theories: no dissonance occurs; and
  \item no agent elects inquiry: $\lambda_iB_i<\gamma_i$, which holds in
  particular whenever $B_i=0$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

Conditions (i)--(ii) are subjective rationality with confirmed conjectures;
they are the self-confirming-equilibrium conditions of the fixed-language
tradition.  Conditions (iii)--(iv) are the new, epistemic ones: the language
survives its own evidence, and the question has no represented value.  A SCUE
with every $\mathcal P_i$ sufficient for $\tau$ and beliefs concentrated near
truth is simply a self-confirming equilibrium; the concept's interest lies in
the other cases.

\begin{remark}[The grain of truth, restored endogenously]
\label{rem:grain}
Convergence results in the rational-learning tradition require a grain of
truth: the true environment must be absolutely continuous with respect to
beliefs \citep{KalaiLehrer1993a}.  Under unawareness the condition fails at
the level of language---the truth may be inexpressible---and statistical
dissonance is precisely the detector of that failure.  Each successful
refinement restores the grain \emph{on the path of play}: post-restoration,
some expressible theory matches the visited conditionals within tolerance.
A SCUE is a configuration in which the grain of truth holds on-path for every
agent and no one has a priced reason to look for the off-path remainder.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Absorption; sketch]
\label{prop:absorption}
Suppose tests are consistent (\cref{lem:test-consistency}), tolerances are
generic, conjectures are empirically updated, and ties in
\cref{eq:subjective-optimality} are broken by a fixed rule.  Then almost
surely there is a generation after which every agent's awareness is constant
and the population is in one of two absorbing regimes:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item a SCUE, in which every agent lives in a Bayesian world; or
  \item \emph{dissonant stasis} for a nonempty set of agents: dissonance
  recurs but inquiry is never both elected and successful---because
  $\lambda_iB_i<\gamma_i$, or because elected production fails
  thereafter---and the affected agents carry forward committed policies with
  standing anomalies.
\end{enumerate}
If, whenever dissonance occurs with $B_i>0$, the election threshold is met and
$\phi_i$ succeeds with probability bounded away from zero, then regime (ii)
with $B_i>0$ is transient and absorption into a SCUE or into zero-leverage
stasis is almost sure.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
\Cref{lem:finite-refinements} gives a terminal refinement almost surely.
After it, awareness is fixed; \cref{lem:test-consistency} implies each agent's
active set is eventually constant almost surely.  If it is eventually
nonempty for all agents, posteriors converge on the finite theory space,
empirical conjectures converge to induced play, and fixed tie-breaking makes
policies eventually constant; the limit satisfies (i)--(iii), and (iv) holds
because a met election threshold with success probability bounded below would
produce a further refinement, contradicting terminality.  If some agent's
active set is eventually empty, dissonance recurs; terminality again forces
the absence of successful elected production, which is regime (ii).  The
final claim iterates: each met-threshold dissonance episode produces a
refinement with probability bounded below, and only finitely many refinements
exist.  A full treatment, including uniform integrability of the test
thresholds across the finitely many awareness regimes, is deferred to the
paper.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Rational anomaly tolerance]
\label{rem:anomaly-tolerance}
Zero-leverage dissonant stasis is not a pathology of the model but a
recognizable condition: the agent knows its causal account is broken---every
expressible theory is rejected---yet the break does not disturb any
represented action comparison, so repairing it is worth nothing.  Standing
anomalies tolerated by working scientists and managers alike have exactly
this structure.  The model locates them: anomaly tolerance is rational
precisely when pinned distinctions have no robust action leverage.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[On-path adequacy, and where error lives]
\label{prop:onpath-adequacy}
In a SCUE, every theory in the support of every $\mu_i$ is $\eta$-adequate at
every visited pair.  All surviving falsity is confined to what the
configuration does not expose: mechanism assignments at states pooled with
causally different states whose mixture imitates a single mechanism at the
visited profiles, and counterfactual claims at unvisited profiles.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Immediate from \cref{def:scue}(iii) and \cref{lem:test-consistency}; the
residual clause is the complement of the visited pairs' constraints.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Persistent performance differences]
\label{prop:persistence}
There exist primitives and absorbing configurations in which agents hold
different terminal partitions and theories, expected per-generation payoffs
differ strictly and permanently, and no disadvantaged agent has a priced
repair: for each such agent either $B_i=0$ or $\lambda_iB_i<\gamma_i$.  The
laboratory supplies instances: an agent with the correct factor refinement
earns three-quarters against a coarse rival's one-quarter forever when the
rival's inquiry cost exceeds its leverage; and under forgiving demand, a
population sharing a false formulation splits the market at pooled actions
indefinitely, each member half-warranted, none dissonant, none with positive
leverage.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The displayed instances are \citep[the asymmetric-insight and trap
configurations of][]{FourStateNote} embedded via \cref{sec:instance}; the
cost-based instance is absorbing by \cref{prop:absorption}(ii) and the trap
instance satisfies \cref{def:scue} directly at the forgiving-demand
parameter.
\end{proof}

The persistence mechanism is the point: performance differences persist not
because an equilibrium concept freezes them but because the process that
would erase them has died economically---the losing configuration generates
no dissonance, or generates no leverage, or prices the question below its
cost.  Heterogeneous terminal awareness is the general model's version of the
laboratory's path dependence, and its comparative statics run through the two
sources of diagnosticity: strategic variety inside one's cells and the
environment's refusal to pay false theories quietly.

\begin{definition}[Diagnostic environments]
\label{def:diagnostic}
The primitives $(\Theta,\tau,\rho,u)$ are \emph{diagnostic} if under every
profile sustainable in a SCUE, every expressible theory that is payoff-relevantly
false---false at some state where the truth would change the holder's
optimal action---is $\eta$-inadequate.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[No payoff-relevant error in diagnostic environments]
\label{prop:diagnostic}
In a diagnostic environment, every SCUE is free of payoff-relevant error:
value-relevant falsity survives only in environments that admit concealing
profiles.  The laboratory's demand parameter is the sharpest instance: its
$\beta<1$ environments are diagnostic and its $\beta=1$ environment is not.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By \cref{def:scue}(iii), SCUE theories are not $\eta$-inadequate; in a
diagnostic environment payoff-relevantly false theories are, so none
survives.  The laboratory instances are verified in \citep{FourStateNote}.
\end{proof}

\section{Cognitional interpretation and checklist}
\label{sec:cognitional}

The mapping to the account of inquiry in \citet{Lonergan1992} and
\citet{RyallWilkins2018} carries over from the laboratory with one addition
made possible by the stochastic setting.  Experience is the flow of episodes;
the question for intelligence is dissonance-localized; heuristic anticipation
is restoration, minimality, and the complexity ordering; insight's product is
the refinement; formulation is the mechanism assignment on the new cells; and
decision is the committed policy.  The addition concerns judgment.  In the
deterministic laboratory, judgment could be identified with fresh diagnostic
labels; here, \cref{rem:self-sealing} shows that within-language updating can
never substitute for it---the posterior ranks expressible answers but cannot
ask whether any expressible answer is adequate.  Judgment is implemented by
the consistency test and by warrant accounting
(\cref{thm:uninformative-production}), faculties formally distinct from the
posterior, which is the model's rendering of the distinction between
understanding and reflective judgment.  The election $\varepsilon_i$ remains
the economic act that prosecutes an unrestricted desire to know under
restricted resources.

Against the no-baked-in checklist: agents hold no prior over the outputs of
$\phi_i$ (\cref{def:technology}); questions are generated by defects, not
drawn from a primitive list (\cref{def:dissonance}); inquiry can fail or
produce false formulations (\cref{ass:elective},
\cref{thm:nonidentification}); leverage bounds are subjective and coarse
(\cref{def:leverage}); identification respects observational equivalence
(\cref{thm:nonidentification}); pre-insight objects are
$\mathcal P_i$-measurable (\cref{def:awareness}); no discovery supergame is
optimized (\cref{prop:participation} prices only the event of success); the
generating record is separated from warrant
(\cref{thm:uninformative-production}); rival behavior is endogenous
(\cref{ass:commitment}); and the representational primitive is stated:
$\Theta$, the recoding capacity, and $\kappa$.

\section{Open items}
\label{sec:open}

The note leaves for the paper, in rough order of importance: the full
absorption theorem behind \cref{prop:absorption}; comparative statics of
terminal heterogeneity in the two diagnosticity channels; equilibrium
analysis where value destruction creates anti-coordination incentives;
characterization of counterfactual transparency and its effect on the
suppression of inquiry (\cref{rem:leverage-qualified}); endogenous
diagnosticity, where the demand side responds to misservice; test design as a
choice variable; Markov state processes; and the deeper dimension of
awareness growth in which the mechanism vocabulary $\Theta$ itself expands.
